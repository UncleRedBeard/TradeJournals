# Learning to See: From Kodak Disc to the Darkroom

## Purpose

Photography is a lifelong companion practice rather than a primary trade offering within TradeJournals. It influences how I observe materials, recognize character, document work, and preserve evidence across the residence, workshop, machines, and pottery journals.

This journal provides the personal chronology behind the later Flickr and Lomography archives. Those platform records remain separate so their photo IDs, URLs, metadata, and access histories stay traceable.

## First Camera

Around age ten, I received a Kodak Disc Camera for Christmas. The photographs preserved in my childhood albums are the earliest surviving pictures I made.

The exact Christmas year and camera model still need to be confirmed from the physical albums, the camera itself, or family records. Until then, the approximate age and remembered format should remain clearly identified as personal recollection.

Kodak introduced Disc film in 1982 as a compact, automated consumer format. Each cartridge held fifteen small exposures. The format's technical limitations are part of the character of the surviving prints, but these photographs matter primarily because they preserve the beginning of the practice.

## Family Influence

My grandfather instilled my original love of photography. His influence predates any formal instruction and established photography as something personal: a way of paying attention, selecting what mattered, and preserving experience.

His camera later became part of my formal education. I still own it.

## Formal Study

Around 1989-1990, I took Wayne Loucas's college-level black-and-white photography course. I used my grandfather's camera for the class.

The course gave formal discipline to an interest that had begun in childhood. It introduced a more deliberate relationship with exposure, composition, film development, darkroom printing, editing, and the evaluation of a photograph as an intentional image rather than only a record of an event.

## Continuing Practice

The practice later expanded across:

- 35mm film
- 120 film
- vintage folding and bellows cameras
- plastic and Lomography cameras
- pinhole photography
- intentional multiple exposures
- digital project documentation

Photography remains both an independent creative outlet and the visual language through which much of the work in TradeJournals is made legible.

## Place Within TradeJournals

Photography is not the principal business represented by TradeJournals. The primary craft practices are architectural preservation, workshop infrastructure, machines, and ceramics.

The Lens supports those practices by documenting condition, process, intervention, material behavior, and finished work. It also preserves a lifelong body of photographic work with its own family lineage, formal training, and continuing experimentation.
