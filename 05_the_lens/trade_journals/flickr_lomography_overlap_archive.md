### El Toro - Pinhole With Lens

All seven Flickr photographs were visually reviewed on October 2, 2026. The sequence moves through an owl figure and weathered rail, a low street-and-curb view, chairs, house exteriors, and foliage. Close foreground surfaces and receding lines give the images depth despite their soft detail, dark edges, and grainy appearance. The owl frame is the strongest representative: the rail's grain leads directly toward a recognizable subject. These are observations of the displayed images, not a diagnosis of exposure, processing, or scanning faults.

The owl frame visually corresponds to [Lomography photo 15964487](https://www.lomography.com/homes/texasredd/albums/1838417-el-toro-pinhole-with-lens/15964487). That page attributes it to a Diana F+ and Lomography B&W 100 (120), with Austin, 2012/dusk, and a pinhole tag. Those labels do not independently establish the optical setup or exposure settings. Shawn clarified on October 3, 2026: "El Toro is a 120 format camera." The catalog now classifies El Toro as 120, replacing its earlier 35mm placement under the general Diana F+ rule. The `(120)` film label was rechecked on that Lomography page the same day; the platform's camera label remains source-attributed. Flickr's NORITSU scanner metadata is not taking-camera evidence. The Flickr album displayed seven images and Lomography ten at the visual-review checkpoint, so the albums are not treated as identical sets.

- Source coverage: Flickr photos `7137622673` (`04940001`), `7137623231` (`04940004`), `7137623509` (`04940006`), `7137623667` (`04940007`), `6991542544` (`04940008`), `6991542746` (`04940009`), and `6991542888` (`04940010`), each inspected at page-display size.

### SXSW 2011 - Part 1

All 17 Flickr photographs were visually reviewed on October 2, 2026. The sequence moves between close portraits, people gathered indoors, bottle-lined shelves, street fronts, and increasingly abstract light trails. Deep shadows, warm red-orange lights, cooler blue-green areas, and softened or repeated contours give the set an unsettled nighttime rhythm. The opening portrait is the strongest representative because the face and forward-reaching gesture remain legible against that movement. These are observations of the displayed photographs, not proof of particular exposure settings or processing methods; performer identities and venue names are not established here.

The portrait visually matches [Lomography photo 12915139](https://www.lomography.com/homes/texasredd/albums/1689964-sxsw-2011-part-1/12915139), whose page lists a Diana F+, Kodak Ektachrome 64T (120), Austin, 2011/night, and `sxsw`. These remain source-attributed claims. The catalog's rule classifying Diana F+ frames as 35mm conflicts with the `(120)` film label and is not resolved by this visual review. Both albums displayed 17 photographs, but only the selected portrait was compared at full page-display size across platforms; equal counts do not establish identical sets.

### SXSW 2011 - Part 2

All seven Flickr photographs were visually reviewed on October 2, 2026. The sequence moves from electronic controls and wired equipment to a street with parked cars, a warmly lit seating area, an illuminated hoop beside a cycle rickshaw, a diagonal blue-white light streak, and a tabletop with a red toy instrument and glasses. Bright looping traces, softened contours, and deep shadows connect these varied subjects. The hoop frame is the strongest representative because the face and large circular form remain readable within the surrounding darkness. These observations describe the images' appearance, not their exposure settings or the cause of blur and color effects; people and venues are not identified.

The hoop frame visually matches [Lomography photo 12915508](https://www.lomography.com/homes/texasredd/albums/1689993-sxsw-2011-part-2/12915508). Its page attributes Diana Mini, Kodak Ektachrome 64T (120), Austin, 2010/night, and `sxsw`. The 2010 year label differs from the album's 2011 title, while the `(120)` film label conflicts with the catalog's 35mm classification. Neither inconsistency is resolved by this visual review. Both albums displayed seven photographs, but only this selected frame was compared at full page-display size across platforms; equal counts do not prove identical sets.
