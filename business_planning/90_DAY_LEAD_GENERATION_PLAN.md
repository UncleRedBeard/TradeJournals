# 90-Day Lead Generation Plan

Prepared for Shawn Kelley
Date: August 21, 2026

## Purpose

This plan establishes a practical first lead-generation system for a
preservation-led restoration practice. It is designed for the current
transition period, when approximately 10 to 12 fragmented hours per week are
available alongside Shawn's existing career.

The objective is not to generate the largest possible volume of inquiries. It
is to create a small, repeatable pipeline of well-matched property owners who
value original materials, careful assessment, transparent documentation, and
phased restoration work.

This is a working operating plan, not a guarantee of marketing results.

## 90-Day Outcome

By the end of 90 days, the practice should have:

- A clearly defined paid assessment with written deliverables.
- Three strong portfolio case studies drawn from existing work.
- A documented external-client proof cycle built around Mimosa's floor
  restoration project.
- A prioritized network of referral partners who regularly encounter owners
  of older homes.
- A simple lead tracker that records every inquiry and its outcome.
- Enough real-world evidence to refine positioning, pricing, capacity, and
  next-step marketing decisions.

## Guardrails

- Position the practice as preservation-led historic floor, interior wood,
  and architectural-surface restoration, not generic handyman work.
- Prioritize owner-occupants who care about retaining original material.
- Consider landlords and investors only when their goals align with the same
  preservation and quality standards.
- Keep Smithville and Bastrop County as the initial core service territory.
- Accept qualified adjacent repair and refinishing work when it supports the
  restoration practice and fits available capacity.
- Use paid assessments with written deliverables as the normal entry point.
- Treat Mimosa's project as a one-time known-client pilot exception, not a
  precedent for waiving consultation fees.
- Do not buy leads or run paid advertising during this first 90-day period.
- Do not broaden into general handyman services merely to fill the pipeline.

## Lead Engine

The initial lead engine is:

> Visible work -> trusted introduction or local search -> paid assessment ->
> phased project -> case study -> review and referral

Each completed project should strengthen the next opportunity. The visual
record, written reasoning, and client experience are part of the product, not
afterthoughts.

## Ideal-Fit Client

An ideal client:

- Owns or is responsible for an older home or structure.
- Values original floors, trim, doors, paneling, or other architectural
  material.
- Wants the existing condition assessed before deciding what to replace.
- Accepts that careful restoration may require investigation and phased work.
- Values written scope, photographs, sketches, transparency, and clear
  tradeoffs.
- Respects scheduling boundaries and realistic lead times.

A poor-fit inquiry is one driven solely by the lowest price, immediate cosmetic
turnaround, replacement without assessment, or a broad list of unrelated
handyman tasks.

## Core Offer

The normal first engagement is a paid on-site assessment that includes:

- Existing-condition photographs and field sketches.
- Measurements and identification of affected materials.
- A written condition assessment.
- Preservation, repair, replacement, and deferral options where appropriate.
- Recommended project phases and sequencing.
- Preliminary labor, material, access, protection, and cleanup assumptions.
- Known risks, unknowns, and items requiring further investigation.
- A written next-step recommendation, whether or not the client proceeds.

The assessment fee should be separate from project labor. Whether any portion
is later credited toward an accepted project remains a pricing decision to be
tested after actual time and cost data are available.

### Mimosa Pilot Exception

The consultation fee is waived for Mimosa and her husband because of the
existing relationship, prior walkthrough, and their direct familiarity with
the quality and process of Shawn's work. The full assessment workflow will
still be performed and documented.

For internal learning, record the time spent and assign a notional value to the
assessment. This preserves useful pricing evidence without turning the pilot
exception into an ongoing policy.

## Phase 1: Days 1-30

### Define the sales process

- Finalize the paid-assessment deliverables.
- Draft a reusable assessment outline and phased-scope format.
- Define the minimum information required before accepting an on-site visit.
- Establish basic fit and disqualification questions.

### Prepare visible proof

Select and prepare three flagship case studies from existing TradeJournals
work. Each should demonstrate a different kind of judgment:

1. Historic wood-floor restoration and finishing.
2. Interior architectural restoration, such as the Ballet Barre Studio.
3. Material preservation and repair, such as the restored solid-wood studio
   door.

Each case study should include:

- Original condition and project goal.
- Constraints and discoveries.
- Options considered and decisions made.
- Materials, tools, and process.
- Before, during, and after evidence.
- What was preserved, repaired, replaced, or deliberately left alone.
- Lessons that would inform future client work.

### Build the relationship list

- Identify 20 to 25 potential referral contacts.
- Mark the first 10 priority contacts for personal outreach.
- Record why each person is relevant and how a referral could help their own
  clients or customers.

Priority categories include:

- Realtors who regularly work with older homes.
- Independent home inspectors.
- Preservation-minded interior designers.
- Reclaimed-lumber and architectural-salvage suppliers.
- Flooring businesses that do not specialize in preservation work.
- Selected electricians, plumbers, painters, and other trades serving older
  homes.
- Local preservation, neighborhood, and historic-property contacts.

### Establish the tracker

Create a simple lead tracker using a spreadsheet or other low-friction tool.
Do not select a full accounting or customer-management platform solely for this
purpose.

## Phase 2: Days 31-60

### Run the Mimosa proof cycle

- Document the complete work area with photographs, sketches, and
  measurements.
- Record household routines, access constraints, and project priorities.
- Select the room or area that creates the least disruption and provides a
  meaningful first phase.
- Produce the same written assessment and phased scope that a paying client
  would receive.
- Begin the agreed first phase when scope, timing, and responsibilities are
  clear.

### Begin referral outreach

- Contact two or three priority referral partners per week.
- Favor individual conversations over mass messages.
- Explain the narrow practice, ideal client, and paid-assessment model.
- Ask what restoration problems they encounter and what makes a referral
  useful or risky for them.
- Record questions, objections, and language that repeatedly appears.

Suggested introduction:

> I'm building a preservation-focused floor and interior-wood restoration
> practice for older homes. I'm not taking every repair job; I'm looking for
> owners who care about retaining original material and want the condition
> properly assessed before work begins. If you encounter someone like that,
> I'd appreciate an introduction.

### Prepare local-search foundations

Prepare the content and information required for a Google Business Profile,
but publish it only after the LLC, business contact information, and service
area are settled. Preparation should include:

- Business description and service categories.
- Service-area wording.
- Business phone and email plan.
- Representative project photographs.
- A link to the public portfolio or case-study destination.

## Phase 3: Days 61-90

### Complete and publish proof

- Complete a meaningful phase of Mimosa's project or bring it to a clearly
  documented milestone.
- Conduct a final walkthrough for that phase.
- Record before, during, and after evidence.
- Publish a concise case study with the client's permission.
- Ask for an honest review and direct referrals after a completed deliverable.
- Do not offer incentives in exchange for reviews.

### Review the pipeline

- Review every inquiry, assessment, proposal, project, and lost opportunity.
- Identify which sources produced suitable prospects.
- Document reasons that good-fit prospects did or did not proceed.
- Compare actual assessment and project time against estimates.
- Note work that created scheduling strain or conflicted with the intended
  practice.

### Set the next 90-day direction

Use the evidence to decide whether to:

- Continue relationship-led growth at the same pace.
- Expand referral outreach into the larger future service territory.
- Publish additional case studies.
- Adjust assessment pricing or deliverables.
- Increase or narrow the types of qualified adjacent work accepted.
- Prepare for limited paid promotion after all advertising gates are met.

## Lead Tracker Fields

Record at least the following for every inquiry:

- Contact name and preferred contact method.
- Lead source and referring person.
- Property location and approximate age.
- Reported problem or desired outcome.
- Initial fit assessment.
- Next action and date.
- Assessment status and fee.
- Proposal status.
- Project status and phase.
- Estimated and actual project value.
- Estimated and actual labor hours.
- Reason won, lost, declined, or deferred.
- Review and referral status.

## Weekly Cadence

The cadence must fit a fragmented 10 to 12-hour week:

- **Pipeline review:** 30 minutes to update leads, next actions, and dates.
- **Relationship work:** 60 to 90 minutes for two or three personal contacts.
- **Portfolio and administration:** 60 minutes for one useful case-study,
  assessment, or operating-plan improvement.
- **Project work:** Protected blocks scheduled in advance according to the
  current project phase.
- **Documentation:** Included inside each project block rather than postponed
  until the end.

Unused capacity should first go toward billable or proof-producing project
work, then referral relationships, then portfolio refinement. It should not be
consumed by broad social-media activity without a defined purpose.

## 90-Day Measures

Success at day 90 means:

- The paid assessment and its written deliverables are defined.
- Three flagship case studies are drafted or published.
- A list of 20 to 25 referral contacts exists, with 10 prioritized.
- Six to 10 genuine referral-partner conversations have occurred.
- Mimosa's project has a documented assessment, phased scope, and completed or
  active first phase.
- Every inquiry is recorded with a next action and eventual disposition.
- At least one review and referral request follows a completed deliverable.
- Actual time, cost, fit, and capacity lessons are documented.

These are learning and system-building measures. Raw lead volume is secondary.

## Paid-Advertising Gate

Do not use paid search, social advertising, or paid lead marketplaces until all
of the following are reasonably understood:

- Assessment-to-proposal conversion.
- Proposal-to-project conversion.
- Gross margin and effective hourly return from at least three paid scopes.
- The characteristics of a strong-fit client and project.
- Which message consistently explains the practice.
- Available capacity to respond promptly and perform accepted work well.

Paid promotion should amplify a working system, not substitute for one.

## 90-Day Review Questions

At the end of the period, answer:

1. Which lead sources produced genuinely suitable prospects?
2. Did the paid assessment create clear value for the client and the practice?
3. How long did assessments, proposals, documentation, and project phases
   actually take?
4. Which work produced the best combination of craft satisfaction, client
   value, and financial return?
5. Which inquiries should have been declined earlier?
6. What language best helped people understand preservation-led restoration?
7. Is current capacity sufficient for the observed demand?
8. What should be repeated, changed, stopped, or tested during the next 90
   days?
