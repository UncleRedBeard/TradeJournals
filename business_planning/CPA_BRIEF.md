# CPA Brief: Restoration Practice and Career Transition

**Prepared by:** Shawn Kelley

**Date:** August 21, 2026

> This is a working brief for discussion with a CPA. It records current plans,
> assumptions, and questions. It is not legal or tax advice.

## Meeting Purpose

Establish a practical financial and tax framework for launching a separate
restoration business while Shawn transitions out of his current Oracle career.
The desired outcome is a clear formation sequence, bookkeeping system, tax
cadence, and quarterly advisory plan.

## Decisions Requested

- Confirm the recommended formation date and sequence for an independent LLC.
- Recommend the LLC's initial federal tax classification and a measurable
  trigger for reconsidering that classification.
- Identify required federal, Texas, and local registrations and recurring
  filings.
- Establish bookkeeping, job-costing, estimated-tax, and owner-payment
  procedures.
- Recommend accounting software and a starter chart of accounts after the
  entity and tax workflow are settled.
- Define a quarterly CPA review scope through the career transition.
- Identify when an attorney, insurance broker, payroll provider, or other
  specialist should be engaged.

## Business Snapshot

- **Positioning:** Preservation-led restoration of historic wood floors,
  interior woodwork, and architectural surfaces. This is not intended to be a
  generic handyman service.
- **Primary clients:** Owner-occupants who value preservation, clear
  documentation, and phased work. Investors and landlords may be considered
  when their goals align with that approach.
- **Core territory:** Smithville and Bastrop County.
- **Broader practical radius:** The area bounded generally by Gonzales,
  Schulenburg, Brenham, and Bastrop.
- **Initial operating model:** Shawn as the hands-on solo craftsperson, with
  occasional paid professional help when scope or schedule requires it.
- **Consultation model:** Paid consultations with written deliverables. One
  known-client pilot is an explicit exception because a preliminary walkthrough
  has already occurred.
- **Evidence base:** TradeJournals records methods, materials, decisions,
  tradeoffs, and visual evidence. It will support estimating, client
  communication, and portfolio credibility.

The transition is being considered as a balanced model rather than an immediate
all-or-nothing move.

| Layer | Current assumption | Confirmation needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restoration practice | Launch part time, test pricing and operating procedures through phased work, and grow toward full-time restoration. | Actual revenue, gross-margin, overhead, and capacity data must be established through real projects. |
| Potential flower-farm operations role | Approximately $1,600 weekly take-home; Monday through Friday, 6:00 a.m. to noon; potential employer-paid family health coverage; possibly permanent alongside restoration. | Compensation, tax treatment, worker classification, benefits, scheduling flexibility, and outside-work policy must be confirmed in writing. |

The planning target is a combined personal take-home of approximately $2,000 to
$2,500 per week over 46 working weeks. This is a transition target, not a
revenue forecast. The CPA should help translate it into required business
revenue, gross margin, tax reserves, and owner compensation.

## Independent LLC Direction

The settled operating direction is to form a separate, independent LLC for the
restoration practice. It should remain legally and financially distinct from
Haley's existing business, The Repair Shop, LLC.

The CPA is being asked to advise the implementation and tax treatment of the
new company, including:

- Formation timing and the order of registrations, accounts, and elections.
- Initial federal tax classification and the financial threshold or other
  trigger for reevaluating it.
- EIN, banking, credit-card, and recordkeeping setup.
- Owner draws, compensation, and estimated-tax reserves.
- Separation of contracts, invoices, expenses, assets, and insurance from all
  personal and other-business activity.
- Referrals to legal counsel and an insurance broker where their advice is
  required.

## Bookkeeping and Advisory Model

- Shawn is comfortable keeping the books during the initial stage.
- Bookkeeping should be outsourced when project volume, payroll, job costing,
  or administrative load makes self-management inefficient or risky.
- Accounting software should be selected after the CPA confirms the entity,
  tax, and reporting workflow.
- The preferred CPA relationship is a quarterly advisory role, not only annual
  tax preparation.
- Quarterly reviews should compare actual results with the transition target
  and identify upcoming tax, cash, staffing, or classification decisions.

## Questions for the CPA

### Formation and Tax Treatment

1. What formation date and sequence best fit the planned part-time launch?
2. What initial federal tax classification is appropriate for the LLC?
3. What measurable profit, payroll, or administrative threshold should trigger
   a review of an S corporation election or another tax treatment?
4. Which federal, Texas, and local registrations and recurring reports will be
   required?
5. Could any planned service, material sale, or pass-through charge create a
   sales-tax registration or collection obligation?

### Accounting and Cash Management

1. Should the business begin on a cash or accrual basis?
2. How should deposits, retainers, progress payments, refunds, and material
   pass-through charges be recorded?
3. What job-costing structure should separate labor, materials, subcontractors,
   equipment, travel, and overhead?
4. What estimated-tax method and payment cadence should be used during the
   transition?
5. How should owner draws or compensation be handled under the recommended tax
   treatment?
6. Which accounting software and starter chart of accounts fit a small,
   project-based restoration practice?

### Labor, Assets, and Benefits

1. What documentation and classification rules should govern occasional paid
   help, subcontractors, or future employees?
2. How should tools, equipment, mileage, vehicle use, and any qualified home or
   shop use be documented?
3. How should health-insurance and retirement planning be handled if Shawn
   moves from Oracle to concurrent farm employment and self-employment?
4. What tax or reporting implications would arise if the farm role is treated
   as employment, contract work, or another arrangement?

### Advisory Cadence

1. What should the CPA review each quarter during the transition?
2. What financial thresholds should trigger bookkeeping support, payroll,
   revised tax treatment, or a change in the transition timeline?
3. Which decisions require an attorney, insurance broker, or payroll provider
   rather than a CPA?
