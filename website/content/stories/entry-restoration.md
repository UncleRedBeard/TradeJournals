# Entry and Stair Restoration

The entry is a small room with an outsized job. It receives the weather, daily
traffic, and every first impression of the house. It is also where the front
door, transom, stair, landing rail, wall finish, trim, and floor all meet. Work
on any one surface has to respect the rest of that assembly.

The archive records two distinct passes. From September 2024 through January
2025, the work concentrated on the door, stair, trim, and surrounding finish.
Floor paper and careful masking kept the circulation route usable while the
existing door panels, transom, balusters, newel post, and dark handrail stayed
in place. The aim was to make the entry read clearly again without replacing
the parts that gave it its character.

A later pass in December 2025 addressed the floor. The worn, darker finish was
renewed into a lighter and more continuous wood surface running from the front
door toward the stair. That change did not ask the floor to look new. It let
the boards carry their age while giving the threshold a cleaner, more coherent
surface for continued use.

The useful lesson is one of sequence. Protection and masking are part of the
craft, especially where several finished elements meet in a tight space. A
door, stair, rail, wall, and floor can be treated as separate tasks, but the
room only succeeds when they are understood together.

The selected photographs document the protected first pass, the stair and
landing work, and the later floor result. Lighting appears in the archive, but
the evidence does not establish a permanent electrical change. The public
record stays with what can be seen: retained architectural fabric, controlled
finish work, and an original floor returned to service.
