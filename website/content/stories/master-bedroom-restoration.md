# Master Bedroom Restoration

The master bedroom archive records two restoration passes rather than one
continuous project. Keeping those phases separate makes the work easier to
understand. The 2023 pass recovered a worn room for use; the 2024 pass refined
the surfaces and finish after the first work had settled in.

The room's identity came from original wood floors, horizontal board walls and
ceiling, tall windows, retained trim, and an existing built-in cabinet. The
first pass worked with those elements in place. Surface preparation moved the
room away from its aged brown finish and toward a lighter treatment without
discarding the boards or changing the room's proportions. An older electrical
box was opened during the work, providing a useful record of investigation but
not evidence of a full rewiring.

By August 2023, the room had returned to everyday use. A year later, the second
pass began from that serviceable condition. The finished floor was covered
with paper and tape. Windows were masked carefully. Wall and ceiling boards
were patched and prepared around the retained openings, trim, and built-in.
The archive also records outlet testing during this phase, again documenting a
specific check rather than an unseen system replacement.

The final photographs show a room that still belongs to the old house. The
boards remain visible as boards, the floor carries its own history, and the
built-in cabinet still anchors the wall. The finish work quiets the surfaces
enough for those original relationships to read clearly.

For a working bedroom, that restraint matters. Preservation is not only about
what survives demolition. It is also about how surfaces are protected during
later work, how repairs stop short of erasing evidence, and how a room is
returned to use without losing the material structure that made it worth
repairing in the first place.
