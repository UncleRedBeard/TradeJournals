# La Ciotat: Harbor and Street

The [La Ciotat album](https://www.flickr.com/photos/boocher/albums/72157626174678654/) moves among boats, streets, and the built edges of the town. This three-photograph selection starts at the harbor, turns inland, and returns to the water. That order is an editorial choice, not a record of the route walked or the order in which the pictures were made.

In the [opening harbor photograph](https://www.flickr.com/photos/boocher/5489412430/in/album-72157626174678654/), close boat bows anchor the foreground. Moored boats and their reflections lead to a continuous row of pale waterfront buildings. The frame holds working harbor details and the town together, with enough space in the water for both to remain legible.

The [street photograph](https://www.flickr.com/photos/boocher/5488818887/in/album-72157626174678654/) narrows the view. Paving, shutters, and projecting signs form a corridor around three people walking away. Its shaded edges and bright opening give everyday passage a different scale from the broad harbor.

The [R1-08767-0000 scan](https://www.flickr.com/photos/boocher/52834975513/in/album-72157626174678654/) returns to overlapping boats, a pale waterfront, and reflections. A broad dark band at the left and softer detail give the familiar setting a markedly different visual character. The source identifies this as a 35mm film image, but does not establish its camera, film stock, exposure date, or the technical cause of those visible effects. The sequence is about how these three photographs hold the same place in different ways, rather than about a single camera or process.
