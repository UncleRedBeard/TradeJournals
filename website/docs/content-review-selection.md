# Content Review And Selection

Status: editorial recommendation for review. This document selects and stages
source material; it does not authorize publication or change the website.

## Recommendation

Lead with three compact restoration stories: the Entry, Guest Bath, and Master
Bedroom. Together they show wood, trim, floors, fixtures, electrical judgment,
surface preparation, and the patience to revisit work when a better result is
possible. Add two quiet supporting stories: Returning to Clay and the Agfa
Isolette sequence. They broaden the portrait of Shawn without competing with
the historic-house work.

Hold the 1964 Vespa as an explicitly active project until the archive can show
reassembly or a stable current condition. Use the shed as context for how the
work happens, rather than as a lead portfolio project. Keep the current Office
and two studio rooms within their existing evidence boundaries.

This gives the first expansion a clear ratio: three restoration stories to two
supporting craft stories. Restoration remains the service signal; the other
work explains the person, judgment, and curiosity behind it.

## Editorial Position

The approved introduction should guide selection and ordering:

> A craftsman's eye. A tradesman's approach.
>
> My work centers on historic homes, interior woodwork, and preserving things
> worth keeping. It also reaches into old machines, pottery, and film
> photography.

The chosen outlook can appear as Shawn's personal statement, without an author
or origin claim:

> A jack of all trades is a master of none, but oftentimes better than a master
> of one.

The broader work should support a simple invitation:

> Have something worth repairing, restoring, or making? Tell me about it.
> Historic homes and woodwork are my primary focus, and I welcome a conversation
> about other thoughtful projects.

This wording welcomes suitable inquiries without advertising every discipline
as a standing service.

## How The Selection Supports The Business

The restoration stories demonstrate practical trade work: protecting occupied
spaces, sequencing tasks, integrating systems, fitting old and new parts, and
bringing a room back into use. They also show craftsmanship: reading material,
retaining useful wear and original fabric, adapting an object rather than
discarding it, and revisiting a result when refinement is warranted.

Clay shows disciplined repetition and sensitivity to material. The Vespa shows
mechanical reasoning and preservation judgment even while it remains active.
Photography shows careful observation and selection. The shed shows that these
skills live in a working practice. Together they make the broader range feel
connected to the restoration work rather than added for variety.

The practical message is clear: historic homes and woodwork come first, while
the same habits can apply to other objects and thoughtful projects. That makes
the inquiry invitation credible without making the website loud, overbuilt, or
indiscriminate.
